# Customer intake: automatic by default, guided choice on request

Read this reference when a customer uploads a bedroom photo and wants a redesign, plan, or generated after-image.

## Core interaction rule

Inspect and reason first. Honor any functional areas the customer specifies; otherwise choose capacity-matched functions automatically and continue to generation. A photo-only makeover request must not trigger a routine questionnaire. These defaults apply to makeover requests, not to a user who requested analysis only.

Do not ask for room dimensions, number of sleepers, bed placement, wall-side acceptance, style, color palette, ordinary movable-object retention, or plant count. These questions create work for the customer without improving the visual result enough.

Ask no follow-up question unless the image is unusable, is not clearly a bedroom, hides a critical opening/fixed element so completely that a believable layout cannot be inferred, or contains mutually incompatible explicit requirements that cannot be resolved without the customer's choice. If the customer requests help choosing functions, use the single guided-choice question below. “直接生成”, “你决定”, “由你推荐”, or a photo-only makeover request all use automatic selection.

## Automatic function selection

Sleep is mandatory. When no auxiliary functions are specified, prefer sleep plus a complete leisure scene in a compact room, adding a separate compact work scene when capacity supports it. Visible resident activities may justify another compatible choice. Extra room does not require filling every possible function: add reading, coffee or a hobby scene only when it strengthens the composition and closes a believable activity. Preserve explicit customer choices instead of replacing them with these defaults. Briefly state the inferred plan and render without seeking routine confirmation.

## Infer before choosing

From the photograph, determine:

1. fixed architecture: walls, floor, ceiling, door/window openings, columns, built-ins, air conditioner and other fixed equipment;
2. sleep surface to preserve: retain an existing mattress's apparent size/type, but allow its frame, orientation and position to change; single-side access and a two-wall daybed placement are acceptable;
3. replaceable items: a large wardrobe and all ordinary movable furniture, storage, lamps, rugs, art and small objects may be removed, replaced or repositioned whenever the finished render benefits, unless the customer explicitly asks to keep something;
4. bed footprint: if the room is empty, reserve a standard **1.8 m × 2.0 m** bed before evaluating auxiliary capacity; if a bed is visible, use its existing apparent size and type;
5. bed orientation candidates: compare the photographed position, both 90-degree axes, a 180-degree head/foot reversal, sliding along each viable wall and every two-wall corner; do not commit until fixed access and full auxiliary use envelopes have been tested;
6. recovered capacity: classify the room as able to support sleep plus one, two, or more complete auxiliary scenes after the best viable bed candidate, fixed openings, minimum human-use envelopes and compact furniture shells are tested; first attempt independent scenes and allow compound anchors such as sleep plus lounge/daybed only as a fallback or deliberate bed-centered choice;
7. style route: use fixed floor/wall color, daylight and architectural character as compatibility clues, then choose the most emotionally specific high-acceptance route rather than the safest neutral one.

Estimate visually. Do not ask the customer to measure or guess dimensions. Use floor modules, window/door scale, air-conditioner size, visible furniture and perspective conservatively; never widen the room to make a chosen function fit. A function passes preselection when its anchor, body posture and minimum entry/use/exit sequence are visually plausible in at least one viable bed-layout candidate using the smallest believable furniture envelope. Compact sequential overlap is acceptable; conventional rolling-office-chair comfort, generous clearances and cleaning convenience are not required unless explicitly requested.

## Guided choice only when requested

Skip this section in the automatic flow. When the customer asks to choose functions, sleep is mandatory and is not shown as an option. State how many auxiliary scenes the image can support, then ask one short multi-select question:

> 除睡眠区外，这间房还能容纳 **N 个功能区**。请选择 **N 个**：
> □ 休闲区  □ 办公区  □ 阅读区  □ 吧台区  □ 咖啡区  □ 梳妆区  □ 健身区  □ 影音区  □ 兴趣区  □ 窗边区  □ 由你推荐

Show only three to six short labels that actually fit the analyzed room and resident evidence. Do not include descriptions such as furniture lists in the question. Do not ask about a third scene until the image analysis has already established that it fits.

For a compact room, usually ask the customer to select one from only the feasible options. For a medium room, ask for two. For a larger room, state the credible number and ask for that many. If arbitrary combinations would not all fit, offer two or three prevalidated function bundles instead of incompatible individual options.

## Automatic style routing

Use the strongest fixed surface as the starting prior, then adapt to daylight and the room's visible character:

- medium/dark wood floor: forest/natural or Chinese/American retro, selected from the fixed lines, wood tone and daylight;
- gray marble or glossy gray tile: black-white or cool restrained minimal, softened with tactile textiles, local warm light, wall layers and plants so it does not become sterile;
- pale wood or yellow-brown wood floor: cream/natural, forest/natural, or youthful bookish retro with darker wood, checks, red accents, plants and layered personal objects;
- mixed or neutral surfaces: choose the family that best supports the selected functions and strongest fixed color/value anchor.

This is a compatibility rule, not a requirement to make all furniture match or to stay neutral. Preserve mixed origins, comfort density, active surfaces and personal irregularity; floor color must never collapse every room into the same cream or restrained-minimal result.

## Handoff after selection

After choosing automatically or receiving the customer's choices, do not ask another routine question. Restate only:

- fixed sleep scene;
- selected auxiliary scenes;
- automatically chosen bed footprint/orientation;
- automatically chosen style route;
- fixed architecture preserved, plus any user-requested keeps; note when storage is redistributed or replaced for the visual result.

Then compare the viable bed-and-function candidates, build the scene map from the winner, and render. If two functions were selected, first try to close them as independent micro-scenes by changing bed wall/corner, head/foot direction, wall-axis position, storage distribution, desk depth, or chair/seat type. Use a bed/daybed compound scene only when those candidates fail or the customer asked for a bed-centered nest. If a selected scene unexpectedly fails a hard geometry/access gate, try these same options before asking the customer again; never silently replace it with a different function.
